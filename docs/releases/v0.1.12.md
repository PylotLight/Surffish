# Surffish v0.1.12

Surffish lets you tuck the mini window against a screen edge, drawer style, and pull it back out with a small handle.

## New
- **Tuck to screen edge**: drop the mini window past a screen edge (left, right, top or bottom), use the new chevron button in its header, or **Tuck Mini Window to Screen Edge** in ⌘K, and it slides off that edge, leaving a small half-round handle showing the page's icon. On macOS, push the pointer into the top of the screen while dragging to tuck to the top (#201)
- **Pull-out handle**: click the handle to slide the window back out and type straight into the page; click again to tuck it. Drag the handle to move it along the edge. Drag the window more than a short way off its edge to undock it. The handle stays above other apps, on every Space and over full-screen apps, and doesn't take focus from the app you're in (#201)
- While tucked, the global mini window shortcut (⇧⌘Space by default) slides the window out and back in instead of hiding and showing it (#201)
- The edge and position are remembered per display, so the next pop out opens against the same edge. Edges shared with another display never dock, and plugging or unplugging a display moves a tucked window somewhere sensible (#201)
- **Settings**: Settings → **General** → **Mini window** adds **Tuck to screen edge** (on by default) and **Peek on hover** (off by default; resting on the handle slides the window out until the pointer leaves) (#201)

## Performance
- A tucked mini window is hidden but its tab is still treated as on screen: it is never put to sleep, discarded or auto-archived, media keeps playing, and a static page idles at about 0% CPU (#201)

## CI
- New `test:electron-mini-window-dock` smoke test in lane A (#201)

## Packaging
- Apple silicon zip only, unsigned and not notarized. If macOS reports the app is damaged, run `xattr -cr /Applications/Surffish.app` on a copy downloaded from the official release
